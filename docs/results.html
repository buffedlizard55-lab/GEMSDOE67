Across the reported-score family the live score falls as the dot count rises and as the raster moves ONTO the visible catalogue. That is the arithmetic of the published metric: with TP_w = sum_g max_x p k, credit is a MAXIMUM not a sum, so extra dots never add credit for a truth cell that a nearer dot already covers, while every dot costs alpha = 0.2 of false-positive mass unless it sits within 300 m of hidden truth. The exact first-order condition (docs/research/metric-derivation.md, inherited from the shared template) is: a new dot is worth adding iff its kernel credit to a previously uncovered truth cell exceeds alpha * DTI. A uniform 5-px lattice over the whole footprint (206,895 dots) is reported by a sibling session at 0.0904 live, which is the same arithmetic taken to its limit. Consequence for this lane: emit unit-value dots, keep the count in the 30k-45k band the family's best occupy, keep them off the catalogue pixels, and let a calibrated marginal rule - not a hand-picked budget - decide where to stop.
