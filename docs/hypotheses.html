1. H67-A — Orientation-supervised along-strike tip relay (THE LANE, IMPLEMENTED) rank 1 cost medium (~6 min CPU for 2 hide-and-recover folds on 2 cores) IMPLEMENTED AND GATED IN THIS CHECKOUT

Layers
All 19 official GeoDAWN bands (mag_anom 1, rtp 2, tmi_hg 3, geod_2ndinv 4, iso_grav_anom_slope 5, tc 6, geod_shearrate 7, geod_dilaterate 8, tmi_vg 9, deq 10, iso_grav_anom_vg 11, det_elev 12, iso_grav_anom 13, tmi 14, depth_to_base_surf 15, ieq 16, cond_surf 17, iso_grav_anom_hg 18, det_elev_slope 19) + structure-tensor cos2theta/sin2theta/coherence and Hessian line response of det_elev, tmi, iso_grav_anom at sigma 1.5 and 3.0 px + GeoDAWN radiometric K and Th + the 9 named 10 m-DEM-derived topo channels.
Physical signature
A learned axial orientation field (cos 2theta, sin 2theta) supervised within 3 px of visible catalogue traces, a tip/junction heatmap (Gaussian sigma = 1.5 px at skeleton endpoints and junctions), and decoding by across-strike non-maximum suppression + hysteresis + an along-strike walk of up to 8 cells past mapped tips while predicted propensity support persists.
Why it finds a fault the catalogue lacks
The catalogue truncates traces where the mapping medium loses resolution: published fault-population work finds mapped lengths come up short because tip zones fall below resolution (about 500 m per fault in one seismic array; >=100-200 m of DEM truncation in a rift setting). A new expert-labelled fault is therefore most likely to be the missing continuation of a mapped trace, which by construction lies OFF the catalogue pixels and along its strike, not on top of them.
Difference from what is already implemented
This repository starts empty (single-line README at commit 8a64f73). Nothing here reuses a sibling surface: the sibling family's best rasters are label-free physical surfaces (H33 multi-physics orientation consensus, aligned drainage-valley chains, scarplet collinearity votes) or Poisson-thinned dot fields; none of them FITS an orientation head to the catalogue and none walks past tips on a learned orientation. The registry comparison in evidence/e5_uniqueness.json is the check.
Named non-fault mimic
Aligned ephemeral washes and fan-head channels (collinear, coherent structure-tensor orientation, apparent tips at the fan apex); secondary: basalt flow margins, deflation/dune ridges, resistant lithologic contacts, roads and canals.
Expected DTI effect
Small positive DTI gain over an isotropic-NMS control, concentrated in the tip-adjacent subpopulation; measured, not assumed - see evidence/e2_holdout_arms.json.
External data needed / obtainable?
None new. Everything is already in data/ (mirrored, hash-pinned).

2. H67-B — Radiometric alteration-halo asymmetry across mapped traces (K/Th and K/Th ratio step) rank 2 cost low (~1 h) NOT IMPLEMENTED (deferred, out of lane)

Layers
geodawn_rad_u8.tif band 1 (K) and band 2 (Th) + cond_surf (17) + depth_to_base_surf (15).
Physical signature
Signed across-strike step in K and in the K/Th ratio, measured with the orientation head from H67-A: hydrothermal fluids moving up a fault alter the hanging wall (K-metasomatism, illite/K-feldspar), so K and K/Th rise on one side of a trace and the Th/K ratio stays lithostatic on the other.
Why it finds a fault the catalogue lacks
An alteration halo persists where the scarp has been erased by alluvial cover, which is exactly the population a surficial Quaternary catalogue omits; the halo is offset from the trace by the dip of the fault, so the highest-probability cell is not on the mapped line.
Difference from what is already implemented
The sibling family used radiometric K only as a scalar corroboration weight and as a meta-regressor of reported scores; nobody measured a SIGNED across-strike K step using a learned orientation.
Named non-fault mimic
Potassic volcanics and tuffs (high K with no fault), arkosic basin fill, and soil/salt-crust variation in playas.
Expected DTI effect
Medium: it is the one physical channel family with a documented hydrothermal alteration signature.
External data needed / obtainable?
geodawn_rad_u8.tif is present locally (mirrored). To replace the mirror with a first-hand copy: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys, northwestern Great Basin, Nevada - https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and (NOT reachable from this sandbox; obtainability therefore unverified here).

3. H67-C — Relay-ramp / step-over bridge: fault-to-fault linkage at hard-linkage geometry rank 3 cost medium NOT IMPLEMENTED (deferred, out of lane)

Layers
labels.tif trace geometry (component endpoints, pairwise offsets) + det_elev (12) + iso_grav_anom (13) + cond_surf (17).
Physical signature
For every pair of colinear trace ends separated by 300 m - 3 km (a relay ramp or hard step-over), test for a connecting diagonal ramp: an en-echelon overlap with a breached relay ramp produces a systematic drainage deflection and a gravity/conductivity corridor along the bridge line.
Why it finds a fault the catalogue lacks
Relay ramps and step-over bridges are the classic unmapped element of a fault population: individual strands are mapped, the connecting structure is not, and it sits in the gap between catalogue pixels.
Difference from what is already implemented
A sibling session shipped 'analog tip/step-over' dots (reported 0.2632) built from an analogue rule, not from measured ramp geometry between component pairs; no implementation here.
Named non-fault mimic
Inter-strand alluvial corridors and through-going drainages that occupy the same topographic low.
Expected DTI effect
Medium; a natural extension of H67-A that reuses its orientation head.
External data needed / obtainable?
None new.

4. H67-D — Basin-concealed basement step: cover-thickness-weighted gravity/magnetic gradient ridge rank 4 cost medium-high NOT IMPLEMENTED (deferred, out of lane)

Layers
depth_to_base_surf (15), iso_grav_anom (13), iso_grav_anom_hg (18), iso_grav_anom_vg (11), tmi (14), rtp (2).
Physical signature
Ridge line of the horizontal-gradient of isostatic gravity, weighted by cover thickness so that deep-basin cells are not down-weighted, and gated by a low second-invariant of geodetic strain (aseismic creep-free) to avoid the mapped active front.
Why it finds a fault the catalogue lacks
A buried fault offsets the cover-basement contact and the density contrast across it without any surface scarp; a surficial catalogue (scarps + lineaments) systematically omits it while the potential-field response survives.
Difference from what is already implemented
Sibling lanes shipped 'buried basement step' dot fields (gems61 family) using an inversion-derived step; the ridge-line-of-gradient formulation weighted by cover thickness is not implemented anywhere in this repository.
Named non-fault mimic
Lithologic contacts and basin-fill facies boundaries (a hard-over-soft contact also steps gravity), and intrabasement volcanic flows.
Expected DTI effect
Medium; highest risk of mimics because gravity gradients follow lithology as often as structure.
External data needed / obtainable?
None new.

5. H67-E — Hydrothermal discharge-line proximity: vents, warm springs and travertine as fault-permeability markers rank 5 cost low once the data is in hand NOT IMPLEMENTED - needs an external source that could not be fetched here

Layers
A point layer of thermal springs / vents / travertine occurrences, plus cond_surf (17) and the radiometric K/Th channels.
Physical signature
Thermal discharge is structurally controlled: warm springs, vents and travertine deposits line the permeable segments of active faults. Distance-to-nearest-discharge, restricted to cells with a MT-conductive shallow signature, is a direct permeability proxy rather than a geomorphic one.
Why it finds a fault the catalogue lacks
Discharge points mark the fault segments that are hydraulically active today, including buried segments with no scarp; the catalogue is a geomorphic/surficial map and misses them.
Difference from what is already implemented
No sibling lane used a discharge-point layer as the primary geometry; the geothermal-propensity rasters in the registry are modelled from the physical bands alone.
Named non-fault mimic
Spring locations controlled by lithologic contacts or by valley-floor alluvium thickness rather than by faulting; historical/anthropogenic wells mis-recorded as springs.
Expected DTI effect
Unknown - cannot be validated without the data, so it is NOT proposed as viable.
External data needed / obtainable?
Named free official sources: (a) DOE Geothermal Data Repository submission 1391 https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391 (mirrored locally as gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv and gdr_volcanic_vents_in_footprint.csv inside 60GEMSDOE, 672 bytes for the vents file - i.e. essentially empty in the footprint, so the layer is NOT viable as fetched); (b) USGS GeoDAWN report https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and; (c) INGENIOUS project page https://gbcge.org/current-projects/ingenious/. NONE of gdr.openei.org, usgs.gov or gbcge.org is reachable from this sandbox (allowed hosts are github.com, api.github.com, codeload.github.com, pypi.org, files.pythonhosted.org, registry.npmjs.org), so obtainability is UNVERIFIED and this candidate is explicitly not viable yet.
